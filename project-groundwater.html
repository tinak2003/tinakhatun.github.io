Project Overview

Groundwater is the primary source of drinking water for millions of rural Indians. Identifying groundwater potential zones (GPZ) accurately is critical for sustainable water resource planning. This project develops a cloud-based mapping system integrating machine learning with multi-criteria thematic analysis and well inventory ground-truth data.

Processing is performed in Google Earth Engine (GEE) for cloud-based raster stacking and model training, with outputs visualized on a Web GIS platform for interactive decision-support.

Methodology

Ten thematic layers were prepared including geology, geomorphology, soil type, slope, drainage density, lineament density, rainfall, LULC, NDVI, and elevation. These were reclassified and assigned AHP weights based on their hydrogeological significance. The weighted layers were overlaid to generate a composite GPZ index.

A Random Forest ML model was trained using field-verified well data (yield categories) as training labels and the thematic layers as predictor features, significantly improving classification accuracy over traditional MCDA alone.

1

Thematic Layer Preparation

Ten spatial layers preprocessed and standardized in GEE: geology, geomorphology, slope, drainage density, LULC, NDVI, rainfall, lineaments, soil texture, and elevation.

2

AHP Weight Assignment

Analytical Hierarchy Process used to derive relative importance weights for each thematic layer based on hydrogeological expert judgment and literature review.

3

Raster Stacking & ML Training

All layers stacked into a single multi-band raster in GEE. Random Forest classifier trained on well inventory data (high/medium/low/very low yield categories).

4

Web GIS Visualization

Final GPZ classification exported and published on a Web GIS platform enabling interactive exploration by water resource planners and field teams.

Key Outcomes

The resulting GPZ map delineates areas of high and very high groundwater potential aligned with alluvial plains, fractured rock formations, and high recharge zones. The Web GIS dashboard enables district water authorities to plan new bore-well locations, prioritize recharge structures, and monitor seasonal groundwater depletion with spatial precision.
