Project Overview

The Sundarbans — spanning the Bengal delta across India and Bangladesh — is one of the world's most ecologically sensitive and climate-vulnerable regions. Rising sea levels, cyclonic storms, and tidal surges make Pathar Pratima block particularly prone to catastrophic flood events.

This project applies an integrated GIS and Remote Sensing approach to delineate flood-susceptible zones using spatial multi-criteria analysis, enabling disaster managers and local authorities to prioritize flood mitigation and evacuation planning.

Methodology

The analysis integrates multi-source spatial data including SRTM elevation, river network proximity, land use/land cover classification, NDWI (water body detection), and coastal distance layers. A Multi-Criteria Decision Analysis (MCDA) framework assigns weighted importance to each layer in an Analytical Hierarchy Process (AHP) to derive composite susceptibility scores.

1

Data Collection & Preprocessing

SRTM DEM, Landsat-8/Sentinel-2 imagery, and river network data clipped to Pathar Pratima block boundary. Radiometric and atmospheric correction applied to satellite imagery.

2

Spatial Layer Derivation

Elevation, slope, flow accumulation, distance to tidal channels, NDWI (water body detection), LULC classification, and soil type mapping performed in QGIS and ArcGIS.

3

AHP Weighting & Overlay

Expert-based AHP weighting assigned to each conditioning factor. Weighted overlay analysis in GIS generates a composite flood susceptibility raster.

4

Risk Zone Delineation

Output reclassified into Very Low, Low, Moderate, High, and Very High susceptibility zones. Ground-truth validation using historical flood records from district authorities.

Key Outcomes

The susceptibility maps highlight south-facing coastal blocks and areas adjacent to major tidal channels as highest-risk zones. Results were structured for use by Block Development Officers and State Disaster Management Authority (SDMA) of West Bengal in evacuation route planning and early warning network deployment. The study contributes to building climate resilience in one of the world's most threatened delta ecosystems.
