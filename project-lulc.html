Project Overview

Dumdum Municipality — part of the Kolkata metropolitan area — has undergone rapid and often unplanned urban expansion over recent decades, driven by migration, industrial growth, and infrastructure development. This transformation has significant implications for urban heat islands, green cover loss, drainage, and ecosystem services.

This project conducts a multi-temporal LULC change detection analysis using satellite imagery to quantify land cover transitions, identify urban sprawl patterns, and generate evidence for sustainable planning interventions.

LULC Classification Scheme

Six primary land cover categories were identified and classified using supervised Maximum Likelihood Classification (MLC) and validated with Google Earth imagery and ground truth points:

Built-up / Urban
Vegetation / Green Cover
Water Bodies
Open Land / Fallow
Industrial Area
Wetlands / Marshes

Methodology

1

Multi-Temporal Image Acquisition

Landsat TM (historical) and Landsat 8 OLI (recent) imagery acquired for multiple time periods. Cloud masking and radiometric correction applied for comparability.

2

Image Classification

Supervised Maximum Likelihood Classification performed using training samples collected from high-resolution Google Earth imagery. Kappa coefficient calculated for accuracy assessment.

3

Change Detection Analysis

Post-classification comparison between time periods generates change matrices quantifying transitions between land cover classes. Net change and gross change computed for each category.

4

Urban Growth Pattern Analysis

Spatial metrics (patch density, edge density, urban compactness) computed to characterize growth patterns — distinguishing infill development from sprawl expansion.

5

Planning Recommendations

Change trends mapped against municipal master plan boundaries to identify unauthorized development zones and recommend targeted green buffer conservation areas.

Key Outcomes

The analysis reveals a significant increase in built-up area at the expense of wetlands, vegetation, and open land. Water body encroachment — a key driver of urban flooding — is quantified spatially. The findings provide an evidence base for Dumdum Municipal Corporation to incorporate remote sensing-derived change data into development control regulations and green infrastructure planning.

The methodology demonstrates how free satellite data + open-source GIS tools can deliver high-value urban planning insights for municipalities with limited technical resources.
